# CTR-inspired Handling: test build

Find **CTR-inspired Handling** in Studio's Mod library. It defaults off and runs only for a local human in offline races. Mandatory online and leaderboard protection remains active. Enable **Mid-air Jump Drift** separately if you want to test buffered jump drifts too. Turn off Automatic drift boost and other speed/physics tuning modules for the initial comparison.

The module uses TK2's normal manual boost controls and visual effects. Drift boost charge contributes 0–2 seconds to a separate reserve model; its fire bonuses correspond to CTR's 8/10/12 steps. A qualifying native landing callback of at least 2/3 second awards one reserve second and selects fire from airtime. A normal boost-pad event adds up to one second with an outside timer. The strongest fire contributes to TK2's boost speed until reserves expire; braking clears it. The optional in-race status shows fire and reserves. `Fire speed scale` adjusts the speed contribution for testing; default is 1.

Driving exposes experimental multipliers for base speed (default 0.85), acceleration (1), ground friction (1), airborne gravity (1.25), and jump strength (0.85). These are provisional settings, not measured CTR conversions; 1 restores each native value. The game retains its steering, collisions, track surfaces and item effects. Reserve gain, consumption, drift/pad/landing awards, braking, fire speed and display options are separately adjustable. These options affect only this module's reserve model, not native button controls.

The optional speed display reports measured kart velocity in game units per second, separate from reserve seconds. It can hide the native percentage, which reflects the game's reserve-related boost calculations. Optional fire effects select among the current kart's available exhaust variants, with a maximum fire-size control from 1 to 10 (default 4); lower fire levels scale proportionally; mappings and colours are not the original CTR assets and may vary by kart. Super-pad identification is not available. HUD and effects need in-game verification.
Known differences: TK2 drift-meter timing is retained, physical pad-contact freeze and CTR super-pad identification are not implemented, wall-hit reserve clearing is not implemented, and CTR's signed 16-bit reserve overflow is not reproduced. A pad's native speed floor or powerup behavior can affect speed. Snapshot/rewind restores clear CTR fire instead of restoring its history. Disable conflicting tuning or automatic boost modules for testing. Do not interpret this as a bit-exact CTR physics port.

Current-build Ghidra evidence and addresses are recorded in [CTR handling evidence](CTR-HANDLING-EVIDENCE.md). The model is based on `ctr-native`'s `VehFire_Increment` and drift-meter branches, converted to seconds; fire speed uses the Speed-class 54 baseline as a ratio, not a proven unit conversion. Each gameplay hook resolves its full current signature, and the game hash is provenance only.

Suggested first offline test: compare flat acceleration; a complete three-boost drift; a normal pad; a jump lasting over two thirds of a second; braking; then a ramp with Mid-air Jump Drift. Watch the status and report reserve changes, speed, controls, and any strange handling. Visible behavior, frame-rate behavior and calibration require user testing; hook resolution and compilation alone do not prove the feel.

The adapter does not set a frame cap or simulate a 30 FPS game. Reserve decay uses elapsed fixed-step time; driving remains on TK2 physics timing. Scene scanning for the speed display is throttled, but this alone does not establish the cause of any reported stutter. The experimental Froggy momentum retention option limits the rate at which horizontal velocity above the active soft cap is lost during airborne physics updates. Downhill speedghost adds adjustable elapsed-time-based acceleration while grounded and moving downhill, up to a configurable multiple of the active fire cap; it also retains momentum against native downhill drag, including below the fire cap, so the added acceleration can accumulate. Uphill deceleration offsets native acceleration. Braking, motor collision processing, steering direction and vertical velocity remain native. It does not generate speed by hopping. Gain speed on a downhill or from another source, then hop to carry it longer. Tune downhill acceleration, uphill deceleration, downhill overspeed loss and the maximum speed multiplier separately. The fire status includes the detected slope and before/after horizontal speeds; slope samples are also logged at most once every two seconds. Adjust airborne overspeed loss per second, or turn the option off. The 0.6 default is uncalibrated; this is an adaptation, not a bit-exact recreation or a guarantee of a particular uphill behavior. Strong sudden velocity loss is passed through to avoid undoing collisions. Test in game, especially on steep slopes, walls and with other movement modules.
